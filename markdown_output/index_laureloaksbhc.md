# **Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center**(2004-present) Dothan, AL

*Psychiatric Hospital/Residential Treatment Center*

---

## **History and Background Information**

Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center is a [Universal Health Services](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/uhs) behavior modification program that opened in 2004. It was previously known as Ramsay Youth Services. The program is marketed as a Psychiatric Hospital and Residential Treatment Center for children and adolescents (4-18) who are struggling with severe psychiatric disorders, mild to moderate intellectual disabilities, cognitive delays, abuse/neglect victimization, and high-risk behaviors such as self-harm, suicidal ideation, running away, or violence. The program has a maximum enrollment of 118 residents, and the cost of the program's tuition is presently unknown.

Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center is located at [700 E Cottonwood Rd, Dothan, AL 36301](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Laurel+Oaks+Behavioral+Health+Center/@31.2040038,-85.3851182,225m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m6!3m5!1s0x88929317e8264893:0x999466f62c96e190!8m2!3d31.2041647!4d-85.3847418!16s%2Fg%2F1tplr2rj?entry=ttu) . The 10-acre campus is situated in a suburban area of Southeast Alabama, just 20 miles from the borders of Florida and Georgia.

Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center offers three types of programs:

***Acute Crisis Stabilization:** This program enrolls children and teens (4-18) who are struggling with acute mental illness or psychiatric disorders. This program is short-term, with an average length of stay of 3-14 days. The program is divided into two units; one for children aged 4-12 and the other for adolescents aged 13-18. The total capacity of these units is 38 residents.
***Residential Treatment Center:** This program enrolls boys (10-18) and girls (12-18) who live in four separate units divided by age and gender. These RTCs have a total capacity of 80 adolescents. The average length of stay in this program is reportedly around 175 days (~6 months).
***Department of Youth Services Unit:** This program enrolls boys (12-18) who have a delinquency charge along with psychiatric diagnosis, mentall illness, or intellectual disabilities/learning disorders. This unit has a total capacity of 16 residents. It is unclear if this unit is still in operation.

When Laurel Oaks Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center initially opened, it was owned by Ramsay Youth Services, which operated programs in nine states and Puerto Rico. In 2003, Ramsay Youth Services was sold to [Psychiatric Solutions, Inc. (PSI)](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/psi) , which changed the program's name to Laurel Oaks. Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center was owned and operated by Psychiatric Solutions until 2010, when PSI was purchased by [Universal Health Services](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/uhs) and ownership of the program was transferred to UHS.

## **Abuse Allegations and Lawsuits**

Many survivors have reported that Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center is an abusive program. Allegations of abuse and neglect that have been reported by survivors/parents include overmedication, verbal/emotional abuse, general mistreatment, sexual abuse by staff and other residents, unsanitary conditions, and violent/excessive physical restraints. Some survivors even report developing PTSD and other trauma-related conditions as a result of their time at Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center.

On July 29, 2008, a 37-year-old staff member of Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center, Jeffrey Scott McCarter, was [arrested](https://www.al.com/wire/2011/08/psychiatric_worker_in_dothan_c.html) and charged with sexually assaulting a 15-year-old resident of Laurel Oaks. According to reports, McCarter was working as a Technician at the program when he allegedly raped the 15-year-old victim inside of a linen closet at the facility. On August 10, 2011, he was found guilty by a jury and [convicted](https://12ft.io/proxy?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdothaneagle.com%2Farticle_1aff2017-c30c-55c0-8488-10b33c68c1b2.html) of one count of second-degree rape. Because of two prior felony drug charges, he faced a possible enhanced sentence of anywhere from 15 years to life in prison. On September 21, 2011, MCarter was [sentenced](https://12ft.io/proxy?q=https%3A%2F%2Fdothaneagle.com%2Fnews%2Fformer-health-worker-gets-30-year-prison-sentence%2Farticle_ae4ce8ac-8cfe-5651-9212-5b36908a3021.html) to 30 years in prison.

On October 16, 2012, a former employee of Laurel Oaks Behavioral Health Center, Debra Terry, filed a [lawsuit](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=12993954392733286758&hl=en&as_sdt=6&as_vis=1&oi=scholarr) against the facility claiming that Laurel Oaks subjected her to a sexually hostile work environment and retaliated against her for reporting the alleged sexual harassment. According to the suit, Terry was repeatedly subjected to sexual harassment and advances by her unit supervisor, Velton Robinson. The alleged harassment began in July 2010 and continued until approximately October 2010. Terry eventually reported the alleged sexual harassment to Laurel Oaks' Human Resources Director, Terry Fiquett, on January 26, 2011. At her deposition, Terry (plaintiff) testified that she did not report Robinson's sexual harassment sooner due to distrust of the human resources department, supported by her belief that Laurel Oaks had fired past employees for “no reason.” Fiquett completed an investigation in the claims but found no supporting evidence of the alleged harassment. Terry's employment was terminated on February 10, 2011 when refused to sign a counseling form based upon alleged misconduct arising from an incident of improper restraint that occurred on January 24, 2011. The Amended Complaint alleges violations of Title VII and state law, namely, that Mr. Robinson invaded her privacy and Laurel Oaks ratified the same, that Laurel Oaks negligently or wantonly trained, supervised, and retained Robinson, and that Laurel Oaks and Mr. Robinson intentionally inflicted emotional distress upon Terry by subjecting her to sexual harassment and retaliation. The outcome of this lawsuit is presently unknown.
