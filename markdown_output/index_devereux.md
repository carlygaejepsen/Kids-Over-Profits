# **The Glenholme School**(1968-present) Washington, CT

*Therapeutic Boarding School*

---

## **History and Background Information**

The Glenholme School (also called the Devereux Glenholme School or simply, Devereux) is a [Devereux](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/devereuxfoundation) behavior-modification program that was established in 1968 on the country estate of the Van Sinderen family. Today, it is marketed as a Therapeutic Boarding School for children, teens, and young adults (10-21) who are struggling with Autism Spectrum Disorder, ADHD, OCD, Tourette’s Syndrome, Depression, Anxiety, and/or various learning differences. The program has a maximum enrollment of 125 students and the program reports that the average length of stay is betwen 12-18 months, but may be substantially longer if the teen is deemed resistant. Survivors have reported that the average length of stay is typically at minimum 2 years, but some residents may be kept as long as 7-8 years. The tuition for the residential program is reported to be between $66,900 and $119,720 per year. The program also offers a day-school which is not residential.

The program is located on a 110-acre former-estate at [81 Sabbaday Ln, Washington, CT 06793](https://www.google.com/maps/place/The+Glenholme+School/@41.6463528,-73.305719,382m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m13!1m7!3m6!1s0x89e78d3a3d1bfebd:0x5b5bf45caead9db7!2s81+Sabbaday+Ln,+Washington,+CT+06793!3b1!8m2!3d41.647125!4d-73.306873!3m4!1s0x0:0xb750ca70bc935af5!8m2!3d41.646553!4d-73.305295) . The estate had been donated to the Devereux Foundation by Jean White Van Sinderen, who had learned of the work of special education pioneer Helena T. Devereux and desired to create a school where children with "special behavioral and learning needs could develop their potential."

The teenagers live in small dorms on the campus called cottages. Some names were Laurelholme, Birchwood, Carriage House, Whiteholme, Holly House and Glenridge. The program also operates a transition program for residents who have completed the program called E3.

## **Program Structure**

Like other behavior-modification programs, the Glenholme School uses a level-system consisting of five levels. The levels are reported to be:

***Phase Three (P3):**This is the first level a resident at Glenholme is placed on. This phase lasts a minimum of 4-5 weeks. They are given no privliges, besides forced interactions with peers.
***Phase Two (P2):**This phase lasts a minimum 4 weeks, until the resident applies for Phase One and gets accepted into a 'trial/test'.
***Phase One (P1):**This phase lasts a minimum 6 weeks, and until the resident applies for the next level. They are given more expectations to follow and must complete more 'goals' than phases 2 and 3.
***Self Support and Management 2 (SSM2):**This level can last indefinitely, as the staff are the ones who decide if the resident is ready for the next level. On this level, they must send an email to any staff members "affected by their bad behavior" every time they do something considered "wrong".
***Self Support and Management 1 (SSM1):**This level can last indefinitely.

If the resident break a rule and gets demoted, they must go back to P3 and start from the beginning.

The program also uses a point/merit system called the 'token economy'. The residents earn either a 'star' or a 'zero' based on how they behave each hour.

The program also frequently hosts seminars and invites guest-speakers, reportedly to improve their image. Many celebrities have visited or publically voiced their support for the school. The Glenholme School was actually the beneficiary of an [event held by Hollywood elites](https://www.looktothestars.org/news/9761-cast-of-silver-linings-playbook-toasted-as-part-of-campaign-hollywood) to celebrate the Academy Award–nominated film the Silver Linings Playbook. Guests included Robert De Niro, Grace Hightower, Harvey Weinstein (!), Dascha Polanco, Cristin Milioti and Isabella Rossellini.

Survivors have also reported that being LGBTQ+ is not accepted in the program.

## **Rules and Punishments**

The residents at Glenholme are expected to adhere to a strict set of rules. Some of these rules, as reported by survivors, include:

*Residents cannot 'elope' (go/run off on your own) and they must remain under staff supervision at all times, except when changing/bathroom/shower (unless they are on safety or suicide watch). While the program claims to respect privacy, survivors have reported instances when staff decided to watch as someone showered, got dressed, etc. without authorization from a supervisor.
*Isolating from others and not interacting is also forbidden, as the resident won't earn points if they do. Talking to oneself is also frowned upon, even during confinement (or as it was called there by the staff, 'lower school').
*Phone and skype calls are extremely monitored and will be cut short if the staff determines a 'negative interaction' happens, or the resident says something bad about the program.
*Interaction between male and female residents is strictly prohibited. They are not even allowed to sit near one another.

Punishments for rule infractions include solitary confinement (called 'lower school'), demotion of level, and movement/communication restrictions. According to survivors, physical punishments have also been used against residents, but the program reportedly tries to keep this as quiet as possible.

## **Abuse Allegations and Lawsuits**

The Glenholme School has been reported by many survivors to be an abusive program. Allegations of abuse that have been reported by survivors include solitary confinement, physical abuse, sexual abuse, and verbal/emotional abuse.

In October of 2011, local police arrested a 27-year-old teacher at the school, Brittany Larson, after an investigation unearthed an ongoing sexual relationship that she had been having with a 16-year-old male resident at the school. She eventually pled guilty under the Alford Doctrine, meaning that she does not admit to the criminal act but received a 10-year sentence, suspended after a nine month prison term. Larson was also sentenced to 10 years probation and registration as a sex offender for 10 years.

In 2017, Glenholme became the subject of another child abuse sex scandal dating back to 2001. A former resident at the program, who was there in 2001, filed a lawsuit against the school claiming they ignored his reports of sexual abuse. The lawsuit alleged that the then-fifteen-year-old male who was a resident at Glenholme was forced into a sexual relationship with an older female staff member, Melissa Lee Houck. According to the suit, Melissa Lee Houck began flirting with Lovell when he was 15 and plied him with drugs and alcohol before starting a sexual relationship. Houck is described in the lawsuit as an employee who escorted students to meals and classes and supervised the boys’ dormitory during the overnight hours. Houck, who was around 25 at the time of the allegations, had sex with the teenager at the school, in her car and at various hotels in the area, the suit claims. When the student reported the crime, Judith Cooper, the executive administrator, denied the affair and took no measures to protect the student. The student's parents eventually removed him and sued the school.

Houck was originally convicted of the abuse in 2004 of risk of injury to a minor involving contact with the "intimate parts" of someone under age 16. Houck was sentenced to five years in prison - execution suspended - and 10 years of probation, in accordance with state statutes.
