# **Timberline Knolls RTC**(2006-2025) Lemont, IL

*Residential Treatment Center*

---

## **History and Background Information**

Timberline Knolls RTC was a behavior-modification program that opened in March of 2006. This program previously operated under the name [Rock Creek Center](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/rockcreek) until a financial scandal led to the program's closure and rebranding. Most recently, it was marketed as a Residential Treatment Center for teenage girls (12-17) who struggle with depression, anxiety, eating disorders, substance abuse, PTSD, trauma, ADHD, BPD, OCD, and self harm. TK RTC has partnered with Meier Christian Clinics, and reportedly were able to provide "Christian based therapy" for those who requested it. The average length of stay was reportedly between 30-44 days, but was sometimes as long as a several months. The program was reported to cost around $1,400 per day.

The program was located on a 43-acre campus at [40 Timberline Dr, Lemont, IL 60439](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Timberline+Knolls+Residential+Treatment+Center/@41.6698698,-88.0127481,367m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x7f76e20f36350973!8m2!3d41.6702415!4d-88.0118115) . This was the former location of [Rock Creek Center](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/rockcreek) , which closed in 2002 amid financial difficulties and allegations of abuse and fraud. Timberline Knolls also offered a residential program for adult women 18+, as well as a partial hospitalization program (PHP) and an intensive outpatient program, both of which were also for adult women.

## ** Founders and Notable Staff**

** Diane Carugati** was CEO of Timberline Knolls at its time of closure. She began working at Timberline Knolls in February of 2010 in various roles, including Business Development Manager and National Director Of Business Development. She left TK in March of 2018, and began working as the Senior Vice President of Operations and Business Development at [Pinnacle Treatment Centers](https://www.reddit.com/r/troubledteens/wiki/index/pinnacletc) from 2018 until 2019. She returned to Timberline Knolls as the CEO in November of 2019.

** Nora Beasanski** was Clinical Director of Timberline Knolls. She has worked at TK since 2010. Prior to joining TK, she worked in private practice, community mental health, police crisis intervention, and MRAI.

** Jami Aitken** was Assistant Clinical Director of Timberline Knolls.

## ** Program Structure**

The program used a point card where the residents needed to get a certain amount of points per day to earn their level, meaning they could have a different level each day. The levels were reported to be:

***S:** This was the lowest level at TK. On this level, residents are permitted very minimal communication with their parents.
***R:** On this level, residents received additional time to call their parents.
***I:** This was the highest level at TK. While on this level, the residents were permitted the most time to speak with their parents.

The residents also could earn something called a "white hat", which reportedly meant they were permitted to go an off-campus trip each week.

While at Timberline, the teens were forced to adhere to a strict set of rules. Some of these rules included not being allowed outside except to travel to and from groups, no going into other teen's rooms, and restrictions of food. The entiree campus was locked with alarms on the doors.

## ** Abuse Allegations and Lawsuits**

Many survivors have reported that Timberline Knolls was an abusive program. Allegations of abuse and neglect that have been reported by survivors include violent and excessive restraints, isolation tactics, LGBTQ+ discrimination, unstable living conditions, mistreatment, verbal and emotional abuse, sexual abuse, ignoring public health guidelines (during the COVID-19 pandemic), severe medical neglect, and more.

In 2018, 33-year-old resident Grace Cho left TK during her intake interview and then took her own life in a nearby hotel. Timberline officials did not alert police until five hours after Cho left.

Also in 2018, TK therapist Michael Jacksa was arrested for sexually assaulting one of his female patients. Five more women also came forward, alleging that Jacksa had had engaged in "inappropriate sexual behavior" during therapy sessions on several occasions between December of 2017 and June of 2018. In April of 2019, the six former residents of TK filed a lawsuit against the program alleging administrators failed to protect them from a counselor who later was charged with sexual assault and abuse. A seventh victim came out against him in August of 2020. All victims appear to have been over the age of 18. In total, 62 charges were brought against Jacksa, including charges of criminal sexual assault and criminal sexual abuse.
