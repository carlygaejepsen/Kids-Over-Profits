# **Oneida Baptist Institute**(1899-present) Oneida, KY

*Southern Baptist Residential School*

---

## **History and Background Information**

Background

Oneida Baptist Institute is located at [11 Mulberry St, Oneida, KY 40972](https://www.google.com/maps/place/11+Mulberry+St,+Oneida,+KY+40972/@37.2678956,-83.6502,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x88435710d01d5a9b:0x3b93461add199a80!8m2!3d37.2678956!4d-83.6502!16s%2Fg%2F11dftppml7?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI1MTAxNC4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D) . The original structure was built by local volunteers on a ten-acre site donated by Martha Hogg. A board of trustees was formed, with members from local formerly feuding clans; more than half of the members were illiterate and signed with an "X." Men from both sides provided money, materials, and labor to construct McMurray Hall, which opened on January 1, 1900.

The school began with about 100 students in grades one through eight and three instructors: Burns, McMurray, and C.A. Dugger. Students, who often could not pay the $1 monthly tuition, compensated the school with labor or bartered goods, such as a sheepskin or a bull. The instructors frequently did not receive a salary and relied on a garden, fishing, and donations for sustenance.

McMurray departed after the first year, and Dugger resigned the following year. Other local individuals became instructors. The school received a $5,000 donation from Dr. and Mrs. J. B. Marvin of Louisville in 1902, which funded the addition of a brick classroom building, converting McMurray Hall into a boys' dormitory.

Burns solicited funds in cities like Louisville and New York. His efforts resulted in donations that financed the construction of Carnahan Hall (a girls' dorm) in 1905, a farm in 1910, and Anderson Hall in 1911.

By the 1950s, OBI described itself as "no longer just a school for isolated mountain children," but "a haven for youth from difficult situations."

## **Founders and Notable Staff**

**James Anderson Burns**is the Founder of Oneida Baptist Institute. Burns (1865-1945) grew up in West Virginia. His family had relocated from Kentucky, reportedly to avoid the Baker-Howard feud. As a young man, Burns moved to Clay County, Kentucky, where he became involved in the region's existing feuds. Described as "big, strong and a skilled gunman," he survived a four-year period of conflict. Following an incident where he was injured and left for dead, he retreated and subsequently decided to cease his participation in the violence and become a preacher. Burns returned to Clay County and founded Oneida Baptist Institute with cooperation from both sides of the local opposing clans.

**Larry Allen Gritton, Jr.**is the current President of Oneida Baptist Institute. He assumed this role in 2013.

The compensation model is reportedly a mechanism for financial and institutional control over staff, preventing dissent or whistleblowing. Staff are paid a critically low base salary of $12,500-$15,000 annually (Employment Cover letter). The school provides housing, utilities, and meals, tying survival directly to employment.

## **Program Structure**

Unlike many TTI programs, OBI does not use a level system. While official materials claim that students must attend voluntarily, the strictly controlled environment at OBI challenges that concept. Boarding students in particular are "never permitted to be around commuting students’ vehicles." The handbook dictates total control over identity, behavior, and family communication.

Denial of Identity: Prohibits dress that suggests "willfully reject one's sex assigned at birth" and engaging in behavior "fundamentally contrary to the Christian faith" (Student & Parent Handbook, Page 15).

OBI actively targets and exploits the vulnerability of international students, historically recruiting on a massive scale (e.g., over 100 Ethiopian students in one year in the 1990s, and using the president of Ethiopia’s son for promotional purposes). These students are then immediately subjected to rules demanding total cultural assimilation, including a ban on speaking languages other than English and using ethnic slang in the classroom (Page 23 of the handbook). This isolation is reinforced by a policy where all mail and packages are subject to inspection (Page 18 of the handbook).

Family Alienation: Instructs parents, "Remember, we don't believe everything your child says about you, so please be careful in believing everything that your child says about staff" (Student & Parent Handbook 2023-2024, Page 27).

## **Abuse Allegations**

The environment is characterized by forced labor, a culture of physical punishment, and documented failures to protect minors.

Forced Labor: Students are "forced to work" (e.g., kitchen, farm) with excessive hours negatively impacting academics (Parent Testimonials).

Corporal Punishment: Survivors allege the use of a "wooden paddle" and "fiberglass paddle at the farm" (Alumni Facebook Group).

Sexual Abuse/Assault: Allegations of "sexual abuse that went on by multiple staff for years" (Alumni Facebook Group) and a 2020 lawsuit alleging the school failed to protect a minor from a peer-on-peer sexual assault (WDRB News).

**2019 Sexual Assault and Lawsuit**

In September 2019, a 13-year-old student was sexually assaulted by a 17-year-old student who entered the dormitory of middle school-aged boys where he was not permitted to be.

The lawsuit revealed that the 17-year-old perpetrator had been suspended in August 2019 for sexually assaulting another student, yet was allowed to return to campus. Despite this prior suspension for sexual assault, the perpetrator had access to younger students' dormitories, enabling the September 2019 assault.

In late November 2019, a journal belonging to the perpetrator was discovered containing 'multiple accounts of the alleged perpetrator's sexual abuse of other students.' House parents interviewed approximately eight students named in the journal, including the plaintiff. This raised serious questions about the scope of abuse and inadequate supervision.

Defendants named in the lawsuit:

-Oneida Baptist Institute

-Claudia Kelsey, Dean of Students

-Richard Brown, Dean of Boys

-Natalie Hill, House Parent

Attorney Ben Wyman of James & Wells PSC stated publicly: "We have a reason to believe that there are other victims in this matter, that there have been other students who have been sexually abused at Oneida Baptist Institute."
