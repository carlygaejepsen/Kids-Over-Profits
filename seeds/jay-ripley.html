Research draft - not for publication. This profile draws on APM Reports, The Imprint, NBC News and the Senate Finance Committee's 2024 report. Harms listed are attributed to programs Ripley's companies owned or ran, not to him personally, unless a source ties him to them directly.

Ripley co-founded Sequel Youth and Family Services in 1999 and led it through its growth to more than 30 facilities. In 2021, as Sequel was collapsing under abuse reports, he founded Vivant Behavioral Healthcare, which bought most of Sequel's remaining programs. He is the clearest example in the modern troubled teen industry of one person carrying a program portfolio through a corporate rebrand.1, 3

Roles and timeline

  • Early 1990s: Helped start Youth Services International (YSI) with his mentor, Jiffy Lube founder James Hindman.2
  • 1999: Co-founded Sequel with Adam Shapiro and a third co-founder; Sequel took over Hindman's Clarinda Academy in Iowa.1
  • 2009: Sequel acquired Three Springs, Inc., including Auldern Academy and five Alabama facilities.1
  • 2011: Became Sequel CEO after the Camelot System of Care deal backed by Levine Leichtman Capital Partners.1
  • 2017: Stayed on as chairman when the private equity firm Altamont Capital bought a majority stake.1
  • 2021: Founded Vivant Behavioral Healthcare, which bought 13 Sequel facilities.3
  • 2022: As Vivant CEO, received one of four letters from Senators Ron Wyden and Patty Murray opening the Senate Finance Committee investigation.4

Business model

Ripley described his model in a 2015 University of Baltimore lecture: good margins come from the daily rate agencies pay per child, and the money comes from controlling staffing. The Senate Finance Committee's 2024 report used that lecture as an example of perverse incentives.5

Sequel relied on public money from more than 40 states. APM Reports found that many of the nonprofit boards overseeing Sequel facilities were filled with people tied to Ripley and former YSI executives, including a banker Ripley personally recruited.1

Documented harms under his companies

  • 1995, YSI: Mystie Kreimeyer, 15, died of a pulmonary embolism after days of untreated symptoms.2
  • 2015 to 2021, Clarinda Academy: Restraint injuries, a counselor's sexual abuse conviction, and removal of all Washington and Nebraska youth. Sequel closed it in February 2021.1
  • 2017, Sequel TSI (Madison, Alabama): Two residents who escaped allegedly killed Van Johnson, 61. Madison revoked the facility's license in 2019.1
  • 2020, Lakeside Academy (Michigan): Cornelius Frederick, 16, died after a restraint. Two staff members later pleaded no contest to manslaughter.1
  • 2021 to 2022: Mountain Home Academy, Pompano Youth Treatment, Forest Ridge and Charles Britt Academy closed after abuse findings.1, 2

After the sale, Vivant used the change of ownership to contest lawsuits against former Sequel facilities.2

Connections

  • Family: His daughter Christine Aron, a former Sequel vice president, signed Vivant's Alabama registration.2
  • Network map: Vivant (founder), Sequel (founder) and YSI (relationship not yet labelled).
  • Three Springs: The 2009 acquisition connects him to Three Springs, the company Rosemary Tippett and Sharon Laney came from.1
