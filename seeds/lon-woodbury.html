Research draft - not for publication. Woodbury is profiled for his role as a referral and trade-press figure, not an operator. No harm is attributed to him. KOP's own Woodbury Facts research draws on his archive.

Woodbury was admissions director at CEDU's Rocky Mountain Academy before becoming one of the industry's first independent educational consultants in 1989. For nearly three decades he published Places for Struggling Teens, the Woodbury Reports newsletter and strugglingteens.com. It was the trade's main news source and directory, and it sold paid placements. He steered families to programs while publishing their press releases, making him the referral pipeline in person rather than an operator.1, 2

Roles and timeline

  • 1960s: Public school teacher.1
  • August 1984: Hired as admissions coordinator, then admissions director, at Rocky Mountain Academy (Bonners Ferry, Idaho), CEDU's sister school.1
  • About 1989: Left RMA after about five years; became an independent educational consultant and founded Places for Struggling Teens.1, 2
  • 1989-2017: Publisher of Woodbury Reports and strugglingteens.com.1
  • 1996: Formed a consulting partnership with former RMA staff member Linda Shaffer.3
  • 2005: Quoted defending CEDU as it collapsed; told The Spokesman-Review RMA's reputation suffered for reasons other than lawsuits.5
  • 2018: Published a memoir series on the CEDU/RMA experience, including interviews about Synanon's influence on CEDU.4

How the newsletter worked

Woodbury Reports mixed news ("Seen 'n Heard"), program visit reports and staff moves with paid advertising. Some profiles were labelled paid placements, including Kreg Gillman's 2004 interview as Provo Canyon CEO. Program leaders such as Gillman, Ken Huey and Brad Gerrard visited his office. Its archive of staff moves and openings is now one of the best records of how people circulated between programs.

He described CEDU and RMA as a "grand experiment" drawing on 1960s approaches "not necessarily proven." In 2018 he framed RMA's founding in terms of A Nation at Risk and criticism of public schools.1, 4

Why he matters

Educational consultants send families to programs and are often the first professional a desperate parent trusts. Woodbury helped build that profession while running the industry's main trade publication, and he came out of CEDU, one of the most abusive lineages profiled on this site. The newsletter did note some industry-wide problems, such as the Hartford Courant's 1998 series on restraint deaths.
