Reedy is a Utah wilderness therapy clinician who trained at Aspen, co-owned Second Nature and co-founded Evoke Therapy Programs. He sat on both the NATSAP board and the board of Utah's Division of Child and Family Services. His parenting books and podcast made him one of the industry's most visible public faces, which is the main reason he matters.2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1990s: Field therapist and clinical director at Aspen Achievement Academy and Aspen Ranch.1
  • About 1998-1999: Co-founder and co-owner of Second Nature Wilderness (Uintas, Duchesne, Utah); also primary therapist, executive director and director of clinical services.2
  • About 2004-2005: Helped buy Second Nature Entrada (Santa Clara, Utah).2
  • 2015: Second Nature Entrada and Second Nature Cascades (Bend, Oregon) were rebranded as Evoke Therapy Programs; Reedy became co-owner and clinical director.2, 5
  • Undated: Board member of NATSAP and of the Utah Board of Child and Family Services.3
  • Recent: Founder and chief clinical officer of Finding You Therapy Program, and host of the Finding You podcast.4

Influence

Reedy's book The Journey of the Heroic Parent is the foundation of Evoke's model; Evoke says it is also used industry-wide to educate parents. He also wrote The Audacity to Be You, contributes to Psychology Today, and speaks at parent conferences and universities.3, 5

He has written on the field's history, including a doctoral project interviewing early wilderness therapy figures, which gives him a role in shaping how the industry tells its own story.5

In 2017 he promoted wilderness therapy in a Boston.com story as families sued insurers to cover it. He described daily hiking and therapy assignments, and said Evoke had never had a participant die.6

Documented concerns

  • Rebrand: Breaking Code Silence says the 2015 change from Second Nature to Evoke followed years of abuse and neglect allegations.7
  • Survivor accounts: Breaking Code Silence collects accounts from Second Nature Uintas describing forced transport, months in the winter Uintas, and lasting PTSD and nerve damage.7
  • Washington Supreme Court: Breaking Code Silence filed an amicus brief in case 101561-5 citing accounts from a 2020 Evoke participant.8
  • Conflict of interest: He sat on the board of the state child welfare agency while co-owning programs in the industry it interacts with.3

Connections

  • Network map: Aspen Ranch (clinical director), Second Nature Wilderness Family Therapy (co-founder), Evoke Therapy Programs (co-founder and clinical director) and Dennis Crowell (fellow clinical director). Dennis Crowell is not Sue Crowell, who came out of SUWS and Aspen.
  • Second Nature co-founders: Devin Glissmeyer and Cheryl Kehl. Kehl was NATSAP secretary in 2004.
  • Evoke co-owner: Rick Heizer, also from Aspen Achievement Academy.
