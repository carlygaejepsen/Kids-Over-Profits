Barker was a former stand-up comedian and recovering alcoholic with no clinical credentials. In 1970 he founded The Seed in Fort Lauderdale, a teen drug program modeled on Synanon. Backed by a federal grant, The Seed became the direct predecessor of Straight Inc. A 1974 U.S. Senate staff report compared its methods to the brainwashing used on prisoners of war in the Korean War.1, 2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1970: Founded The Seed, Inc. (incorporated September 23, 1970) in Fort Lauderdale.1
  • February 1, 1972: The Seed's 10-year National Institute of Mental Health Narcotic Addict Rehabilitation Branch grant began, starting at $177,000 a year. The same day, presidential candidate Sen. Edmund Muskie visited.5
  • February 1972: Barker flew to New Hampshire to stand beside Muskie as a witness at the "Canuck letter" press conference, which made him a national figure.5
  • April 1972: Sammy Davis Jr. visited The Seed on behalf of the Nixon administration's drug policy office.5
  • 1970s: Expanded to Fort Lauderdale, Fort Myers, Dade County and St. Petersburg.1
  • 1974: The Senate Judiciary subcommittee staff report on behavior modification criticized The Seed. Federal money dried up and the program shrank.2
  • 1976: Straight Inc. opened in St. Petersburg, founded by Seed parents including Mel and Betty Sembler.1
  • 2001: Barker retired and dissolved The Seed on October 1.1

Methods

Barker built The Seed on Synanon's attack-therapy model. Newcomers sat in "raps" for 12 hours a day, seven days a week, pushed to confess drug use and wrongdoing. Accounts describe sleep deprivation, restricted bathroom access, and children sent home each night with "oldcomer" host families. Staff were unlicensed former clients.2, 3

By January 1972 The Seed had about 600 active clients, ages roughly 10 to 25. Barker told early attendees he took his basic steps from AA.5

Why he matters

The Seed is the bridge between Synanon's adult commune and the teen "tough love" programs. Straight Inc. copied its model and spread it nationally, and Straight in turn spawned KIDS (Miller Newton) and other spin-offs. The federal grant and the 1972 national press made The Seed a template that government officials actively promoted.1, 2
