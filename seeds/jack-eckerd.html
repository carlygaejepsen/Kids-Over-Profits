Eckerd was the drugstore magnate and Republican politician who founded Eckerd Youth Alternatives in 1968. It began as a Florida wilderness camp for delinquent boys and grew into a multi-state contractor for juvenile justice and, later, foster care. In 1982 Florida made Eckerd's organization the first private juvenile justice provider in state history. He is the industry's clearest example of philanthropy opening the door to privatized youth corrections.1, 2, 4

Roles and timeline

  • 1952 onward: Built the Eckerd drugstore chain from three Florida stores into a national company.1
  • 1968: With his wife Ruth, founded Camp E-How-Kee in Brooksville, Florida, modeled on Campbell Loughmiller's Salesmanship Club wilderness camps in Texas; Loughmiller's associates helped set it up.2
  • 1970, 1974, 1978: Ran for governor in 1970 and 1978 (Republican nominee in 1978, lost to Bob Graham) and for U.S. Senate in 1974.1
  • 1981: Graham named him chairman of Florida's PRIDE, the private board that runs state prison industries.1
  • 1982: Florida contracted Eckerd for the state's first private juvenile justice program.4
  • 1999: Eckerd ran 17 wilderness camps in six states plus 16 Florida juvenile justice and early intervention programs, serving 5,500 youth.5
  • 2004: Died May 19 in Clearwater, Florida; buried at Camp E-How-Kee.3

His stated philosophy

Eckerd said he wanted to "turn tax users into tax payers," first through prison job training and then by reaching youth earlier. He cast his camps as a humane alternative to Florida's state training schools, which had isolation cells and attack dogs. The camps billed their approach as "Roughing it Easy."2, 4

Documented harms at Eckerd programs

  • Camp E-Kel-Etu (Ocala National Forest): Michael Ibarra died at the camp. In 2001 his mother filed a $13.2 million federal suit against Eckerd and a counselor, alleging cruel and unusual punishment and that taking him off his ADHD medication foreseeably endangered him.6
  • Foster care (Eckerd Kids / Eckerd Connects): As lead foster care agency for Hillsborough, Pinellas and Pasco counties, it had foster children sleeping in offices and left unsupervised. In 2021 Florida declined to renew its contracts, and the Pinellas sheriff opened a criminal investigation into "institutional abuse" involving top executives.7

Connections

  • Rebrands: Eckerd Youth Alternatives became Eckerd Kids, then Eckerd Connects.
  • Wilderness lineage: Campbell Loughmiller (Salesmanship Club, Texas), Ken Eggert, Buford McKenzie and Everett Lindstrom.2
  • NATSAP: Andy Anderson, an Eckerd vice president and spokesman, appears to be the Andy Anderson who became NATSAP's executive director in 2001 (unconfirmed).
