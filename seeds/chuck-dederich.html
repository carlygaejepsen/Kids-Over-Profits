Dederich founded Synanon in 1958. Its confrontation method, "the Game," became the template for the attack therapy used across the troubled teen industry, including at CEDU, Elan, The Seed and Straight. Synanon turned into a violent religious group, and in 1980 Dederich pleaded no contest to conspiring to murder a lawyer with a rattlesnake.1, 2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1950s: A recovering alcoholic and AA member, he began holding group sessions in Santa Monica.2
  • 1958: Incorporated the Synanon Foundation; served 25 days in jail for running a "hospital" in a residential area.1
  • 1960s: Synanon was widely praised and copied, especially for the Game.2
  • 1970s: Declared Synanon a religion and moved it to the Sierra Nevada foothills. Members were required to shave their heads.2
  • 1975-1978: Lawyer Paul Morantz later described a "reign of terror" of beatings and intimidation by Synanon members.3
  • 1978: Morantz, who had won a judgment against Synanon for defectors, was bitten by a four-foot rattlesnake left in his mailbox. Dederich was arrested in December in Lake Havasu City, Arizona.1, 3
  • 1980: Pleaded no contest along with two members of Synanon's security force. Sentenced to five years' probation, on condition that he give up control of Synanon.1, 2
  • 1991: Synanon disbanded for financial reasons.2
  • 1997: Died February 28 in Visalia, California.1, 2, 3

The method

The Game was a group session in which members attacked each other verbally to break down defenses. Dederich called it tough love and likened Synanon to a firm but loving family. He also decided members could never fully recover, so the two-year program became a lifelong commune.2

These ideas spread through therapeutic communities such as Daytop and Phoenix House, and into teen programs. Former Synanon members and admirers built programs on the same model: confrontation groups, total control of contact, and no graduation until staff decide.

In a recording subpoenaed in the rattlesnake case, Dederich is reported to have said, "Don't mess with us - you can get killed dead, physically dead."3

Connections

  • Programs descended from Synanon: The Seed (Art Barker), Straight Inc. (Mel and Betty Sembler), KIDS (Miller Newton), Elan (Joe Ricci) and CEDU (Mel Wasserman). Each tie needs its own citation.
  • Network map: Synanon (founder and leader) and Alcoholics Anonymous (member). The Synanon frame also holds the Argiros family, the Semblers and Miller Newton.
