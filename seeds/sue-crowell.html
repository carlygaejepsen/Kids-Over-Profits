Crowell ran SUWS of Idaho, rose to Senior Vice President of Outdoor Programs at Aspen Education Group, and is listed as an investor in the Family Help & Wellness (FHW) companies through PGO, LLC. FHW owned Trails Carolina, where a 12-year-old died in February 2024. Her career connects the Aspen wilderness era to FHW, the company that grew out of it.1, 5

Roles and timeline

  • Undated: Executive director of the SUWS Adolescent Program / SUWS of Idaho.1
  • About 2004: Senior Vice President of Outdoor Programs at Aspen Education Group, based in Asheville, North Carolina, per an April 8, 2004 Aspen marketing and program contact list.5
  • 2016: Founded Skyterra Wellness Retreat in Lake Toxaway, North Carolina, a weight-loss and wellness retreat, with co-owner Teresa Ostler.6
  • As of October 2024: PGO, LLC, associated with Crowell, is listed as a member of an FHW holding entity named in the Trails lawsuits.1

In the membership entry reproduced in John Doe M.D.Y. v. Trails Academy, LLC, et al., PGO, LLC is listed with Sue Crowell and this career description: "executive director SUWS Adolescent Program & SUWS of Idaho, VP of Outdoor Therapy Programs Aspen Education Group, Senior VP Aspen Education Group, founder Skyterra Wellness Retreat." Why she matters

As Aspen's senior executive over outdoor programs, she oversaw its wilderness portfolio during its expansion. That portfolio included SUWS, Aspen Achievement Academy and Adirondack Leadership Expeditions. Bain Capital bought Aspen through CRC Health in 2006, and Aspen closed SUWS of Idaho in 2013.3, 5

After Aspen, former Aspen leaders formed FHW. Founder Tim Dupell was Aspen's CFO from 1999 to 2004, and Crowell is among the investors the complaints list.

Documented harms at programs she was tied to

  • SUWS of Idaho: Two teenagers died while attending, in 1985 and 2006. Whether either death fell during her time as executive director is not established.3
  • Trails Carolina (FHW): A 12-year-old boy died in February 2024 after one night. North Carolina ordered all children removed, and the program later closed.
  • FHW class actions: Two October 2024 suits in the Western District of North Carolina name the FHW entities and seek tuition refunds and damages on behalf of FHW families nationwide.1, 2

Keep the entities distinct: the listing does not establish that Crowell personally owned the holding company, managed Trails, participated in the alleged conduct or bears legal responsibility. The complaints' claims remain allegations unless a court or regulator has made a finding.1, 2

Connections

  • Family: Her spouse, Graham Shannonhouse, founded Trails Carolina and was its executive director, and earlier was program director of SUWS of Idaho. Shannonhouse's sister Kathryn Huffman was a founding partner and admissions director of Asheville Academy.
