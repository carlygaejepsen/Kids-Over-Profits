Research draft - not for publication. Wilkerson died in 2011. Lawsuits and enforcement actions since then are attributed to Teen Challenge programs, not to him, and are allegations unless a court has ruled.

Wilkerson was a Pentecostal preacher who founded Teen Challenge in Brooklyn in 1958 and opened its first center in 1960. Teen Challenge, now an Assemblies of God ministry, became the largest faith-based residential program in the world. Its model of conversion as treatment and labor as rehabilitation, run by staff without clinical training, spread through religious exemptions that Texas created for Teen Challenge in the 1990s.1, 2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1931: Born in Hammond, Indiana, to a family of preachers.1
  • February 1958: As a 26-year-old pastor from Philipsburg, Pennsylvania, disrupted a New York murder trial to reach seven teen gang members.4
  • 1958: Began the street ministry to gang members and addicts that became Teen Challenge.2
  • 1960: Opened the first Teen Challenge center, on Clinton Avenue in Brooklyn.3
  • 1962-1963: Co-wrote The Cross and the Switchblade, which sold tens of millions of copies; filmed in 1970 with Pat Boone.2
  • 1971: Founded World Challenge, a global evangelistic ministry.3
  • 1987: Founded Times Square Church in New York.
  • April 27, 2011: Died in a car crash near Cuney, Texas.1

Scale

Earlier counts put Teen Challenge at about 250 centers in 48 states and more than 1,000 in 95 countries; a 2025 count put it at over 1,400 centers in 140 countries. Teen Challenge USA operates as a missionary department of the Assemblies of God.3

The religious exemption

In 1995, Texas drug-treatment auditors cited violations at Teen Challenge and demanded it meet health and safety standards to keep its license. Gov. George W. Bush intervened and promised licensing for faith-based programs would change.5

In 1996 Bush created a Task Force on Faith-Based Programs. In 1997 Texas passed HB 2481, exempting faith-based drug programs from state licensing, and HB 2482, letting faith-based children's homes be accredited privately instead. Roloff Homes director Wiley Cameron helped draft and lobby for HB 2482. Bush later took the model national as his Faith-Based Initiative.5

The Teen Challenge fight is how the modern religious exemption was built, and it reopened Texas to the Roloff homes.

Documented harms at Teen Challenge

  • Central Indiana Teen Challenge (now Refuge Girls Academy): A federal suit filed by nine women under the Trafficking Victims Protection Reauthorization Act, amended June 16, 2026 to 35 plaintiffs, alleges forced unpaid labor of four or more hours a weekday, including at Butler and Purdue universities, plus physical and spiritual abuse, and no licensed teachers or therapists.6
  • Adult & Teen Challenge Northeast: In September 2026 the Massachusetts attorney general accused it of making more than 200 men perform unpaid work for private businesses.7
  • Reputation: A 2021 New Yorker investigation called Teen Challenge a "shadow penal system" for struggling kids.8

Connections

  • Political allies: George W. Bush; Henry Lozano, director of Teen Challenge in California, became a deputy assistant to President Bush.
  • Roloff lineage: Wiley Cameron and the TACCCA accreditation body.5
  • Converts: Nicky Cruz, the gang member at the center of The Cross and the Switchblade.
