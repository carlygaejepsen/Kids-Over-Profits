Kay was president and public voice of WWASPS, Robert Lichfield's network of behavior modification programs, from 1998 into its collapse in the late 2000s. He rose from night staff at Brightway Adolescent Hospital to directing it, then defended WWASPS publicly against abuse lawsuits. His son Jay Kay ran Tranquility Bay in Jamaica.1, 2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • Early career: Night staff, then director of Brightway Adolescent Hospital (St. George, Utah) until it closed in 1998.1
  • 1998: Replaced Karr Farnsworth as WWASPS president.1
  • Before 2000: Briefly left, then rejoined WWASPS as vice president in March 2000.1
  • Undated: Program director at Cross Creek Programs (La Verkin, Utah), according to the network map.
  • 2006: Called a lawsuit over abuse allegations "preposterous" and predicted WWASPS would counter-sue.2
  • July 2007: Told The Salt Lake Tribune only two schools remained in the WWASPS network.3
  • Later: Superintendent of Browning Distance Learning Academy, Lichfield's homeschool curriculum company.3

Public role

Kay was the face WWASPS put forward when abuse allegations surfaced. He told an interviewer that lawsuits were ploys for money brought by people who "are never going to be happy." In 2006 he said the accusers were a few former students "with very troubled histories," and promised a counter-suit.2

A 2007 suit, filed for 133 plaintiffs alleging physical and sexual abuse, drew national attention because Lichfield co-chaired Mitt Romney's Utah fundraising committee. WWASPS and its principals repeatedly sued critics.3

Programs under his leadership

  • Brightway Adolescent Hospital: Closed in 1998 while he was director.1
  • Cross Creek Programs: Founded in 1988 by Lichfield and Brent Facer. A 2006 lawsuit alleged widespread physical and psychological abuse.2
  • WWASPS network: Included Tranquility Bay (Jamaica), Casa by the Sea (Mexico), Dundee Ranch (Costa Rica) and Spring Creek Lodge (Montana), many closed after government raids or abuse reports.3

Connections

  • Family: Son Jay Kay (Tranquility Bay).
  • WWASPS leadership: Robert Lichfield, Karr Farnsworth and Narvin Lichfield.
  • Cross Creek: Ron Garrett, described in survivor accounts as an administrator who reported directly to Kay.1
  • Network map: Brightway (director), Cross Creek (program director), WWASPS (president), Browning Distance Learning Academy (superintendent) and Jay Kay (son).
